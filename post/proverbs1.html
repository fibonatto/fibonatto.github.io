Proverbs 1: The Father Who Teaches Before the Mistake

Proverbs begins by explaining why it was written: to teach wisdom, instruction, good judgment, justice, fairness, and righteousness. It isn’t just about teaching someone to know more, but to learn how to live.

And it starts with something very simple:

“The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom.”

Before any advice about how to act, there is a relationship that needs to be in the right place. Wisdom begins when we recognize who God is and that He has the authority to teach us how we should live.

Right after that, the text says:

“Listen, my son, to your father's instruction, and do not forsake your mother's teaching.”

This isn’t there by accident. The first exercise in wisdom presented in the chapter is knowing how to listen.

The text doesn’t assume that the son already knows what to do. It assumes exactly the opposite: that he needs to be taught.

1. What is God teaching us?

The first point is that the warning comes before the mistake, not after it.

The father doesn’t wait for his son to go down the wrong path and then explain what happened. He tells him beforehand:

“My son, if sinners entice you, do not give in to them.”

First comes the instruction. Then comes the situation where that instruction will be needed.

This sounds obvious, but it is an important difference. Instruction doesn’t exist only to explain why something went wrong. It also exists so that you don’t have to discover it after you’ve already been hurt.

It’s like a father telling his son, “don’t go that way” before the son gets lost, instead of yelling at him after he is already lost.

That is why accepting correction isn’t weakness.

The chapter begins by saying that the wise listen and increase their knowledge. The fool, on the other hand, despises wisdom and instruction.

The difference isn’t that one knows everything and the other knows nothing.

It’s that one is willing to learn and the other thinks he doesn’t need to.

You don’t have to always be right to be wise. You need to be able to listen when someone who knows more about something is trying to teach you.

And that is especially important when the instruction comes from someone who loves you.

Not all suffering is the consequence of a wrong choice

In the next part, Solomon gives a concrete example.

The sinners call the son to join them in an ambush. They want to kill an innocent person and divide whatever they steal among themselves. The promise is easy money, belonging, and a share of whatever they get.

The problem is that they think they are setting a trap for someone else when they are actually setting one for themselves:

“They lie in wait for their own blood; they ambush only themselves.”

The text is showing a real connection between that choice and that consequence. Someone who chooses to follow that path of violence and greed is walking toward his own destruction.

But that doesn’t mean that every pain is the result of a wrong choice.

The text isn’t saying that when someone suffers, it is because they did something to deserve it.

Sometimes life simply hurts. Sometimes someone suffers without doing anything to cause it. Suffering is not automatically punishment.

What Proverbs is teaching here is something more specific: there are choices that really do lead to bad places, and you don’t have to wait until you get there before believing the warning.

Wisdom calls before the consequence

After that, Wisdom appears speaking in the streets, in the public squares, and at the city gates.

She calls.

She reaches out her hand.

She offers her counsel.

And only after all of that comes the hard part of the passage:

“Because you refused my call and no one paid attention when I stretched out my hand...”

This matters because it shows that the consequence does not come without warning.

God calls first.

He warns first.

He reaches out His hand first.

He isn’t someone silently waiting to see whether you will make a mistake so He can then say, “I told you so.”

The hand was already extended.

The invitation had already been made.

The problem is when someone keeps refusing.

That is why the chapter can speak so harshly about those who despise wisdom. It isn’t someone being punished simply because they didn’t know. It is someone who was called, warned, and advised, but chose to keep rejecting it.

And even then, the chapter ends by saying:

“But whoever listens to me will live in safety and be at ease, without fear of harm.”

Safety doesn’t mean that nothing bad will ever happen.

Proverbs isn’t promising a life without suffering.

It is a different kind of safety.

It is the safety of someone who listens before entering the wrong path. And when she has to go through something difficult, she doesn’t have to walk through it alone.

2. What does this text reveal about God?

First, that He isn’t indifferent to the path she chooses.

He doesn’t wait for her to do something wrong and only then care about what happened.

He speaks first.

He calls first.

He warns first.

This appears several times in the chapter. The father instructs the son before the temptation. Wisdom calls before the calamity. The hand is extended before the consequence.

There is an initiative from God here.

He isn’t simply waiting to judge the result. He cares about the path.

And this also changes the way we understand correction.

The hardest part of the chapter is when Wisdom says that she will laugh at the disaster of those who rejected her counsel and will not answer when they call on her.

It is a difficult passage.

But the text itself doesn’t present this as abandonment for no reason.

Wisdom called.

She reached out her hand.

She gave counsel.

She warned them.

They refused.

And they kept refusing.

There is a kind of pain in this: the pain of warning someone you love about the path they are taking and watching them continue in that direction.

It is the same thing that happens when a father sees his son putting himself in danger.

He doesn’t stay silent because he loves him.

He speaks because he loves him.

Correction isn’t the opposite of care. It is one of the ways care expresses itself.

And the chapter doesn’t end with the hardest part.

It ends with the one who listens living in safety.

The last word isn’t “you will be destroyed.”

It is:

“Whoever listens to me...”

There is still an invitation to listen.

3. What does this text reveal about how God cares for His children?

The chapter uses a very specific image to talk about this.

“My son.”

God isn’t presented here as someone distant, simply making rules for people who have to obey them.

The image is of a father teaching his son.

And a father does this because his son doesn’t know everything yet.

So he teaches him.

His son still can’t see every danger.

So he warns him.

His son can still make the wrong choice.

So he corrects him.

This means that correction isn’t rejection.

A father who sees his son running around with a knife in his hand isn’t trying to control his life when he tells him to stop. He is trying to keep him from getting hurt.

A father’s love isn’t letting his son do whatever he wants.

It is caring enough to say no when the path he is taking will hurt him.

And there is something else important here: God doesn’t expect her to already know everything.

Proverbs was written for the simple, for the young, for those who need to gain discernment. Even the wise are called to keep listening so they can increase their knowledge.

So she doesn’t need to have all the answers.

She doesn’t need to know how to recognize every danger on her own.

She doesn’t need to learn everything in the most painful way possible.

She can simply listen.

She can accept instruction.

She can be corrected.

She can change direction before it is too late.

That is exactly what the chapter is teaching when it puts a father’s instruction at the beginning of everything.

Wisdom begins with the fear of the Lord, but it also shows itself in the willingness to listen.

And perhaps that is the most important thing to take from this chapter:

God doesn’t wait for her to get lost before teaching her the way back. He teaches her the way beforehand.

He warns her before it hurts.

He corrects her before the consequence becomes greater.

He calls her before it is too late.

And when He says that whoever listens to Him will live in safety, He isn’t promising that she will never suffer.

He is saying that she doesn’t have to walk through life alone.
